# Conversation Design

## Session shape

1. Set a small goal and scenario.
2. Offer a friendly opening prompt.
3. Exchange several short, realistic turns.
4. Correct immediately only if meaning is blocked.
5. Pause at a natural checkpoint for praise and one or two corrections.
6. Invite a retry or continue with a slightly harder turn.
7. Close with a transcript, feedback summary, and next practice suggestion.

## Modes

- **Daily Conversation:** routines, family, food, and local life.
- **Workplace English:** introductions, meetings, updates, and requests.
- **Job Interview:** common questions, examples, and professional clarity.
- **Travel:** directions, tickets, accommodation, and disruptions.
- **Shopping:** asking, comparing, negotiating, and resolving issues.
- **Doctor Visit:** describing symptoms and understanding simple instructions; never medical diagnosis.
- **Telephone Conversation:** openings, clarification, message-taking, and closing.
- **Free Talk:** learner-led topics with lightweight guardrails.

The tutor adapts vocabulary, turn length, prompt support, and ambiguity to level. Telugu support is a presentation capability selected from learner preference and observed confusion, not embedded in scenario rules.

## Voice and fallback

Voice is the default interaction when available. Text input follows the same turn contract, keeps sessions usable in noisy or low-bandwidth conditions, and helps distinguish language errors from uncertain transcription.
