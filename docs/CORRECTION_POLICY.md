# Correction Policy

## Core rule

Do not stop the learner after every mistake. Preserve conversation flow and confidence.

## Intervention

Correct immediately only when meaning is unclear, the misunderstanding compounds the scenario, or safety requires clarification. Otherwise collect candidate feedback and wait several turns or for a natural checkpoint.

At each checkpoint:

1. Start with specific positive feedback.
2. Select one or two high-impact, level-appropriate patterns.
3. Show the learner's sentence and a natural corrected sentence.
4. Give one concise explanation and, on request or apparent confusion, a concise Telugu explanation.
5. Invite a low-pressure retry.

Priority is: comprehensibility, recurring grammar pattern, scenario-useful vocabulary, then fluency. Avoid correcting harmless variation.

Pronunciation feedback is given only when the speech provider's confidence and audio quality are sufficient. It should target intelligibility, identify uncertainty, and never judge an accent. Low-confidence transcription produces a clarification request, not a learner correction.
