# Learner Personas

## Anusha — college student

Anusha, 19, understands classroom English but avoids speaking. She wants short daily practice, Telugu clarification when stuck, and visible confidence gains. She may use a low-cost phone and inconsistent network.

## Ravi — job seeker

Ravi, 25, can form simple sentences but freezes in interviews. He needs structured interview and workplace scenarios, practical vocabulary, and correction of recurring high-impact errors without constant interruption.

## Lakshmi — working professional

Lakshmi, 33, reads and writes English at work but hesitates in meetings and calls. She needs concise practice, professional phrasing, fluency feedback, and progress that respects limited time.

## Secondary needs

Students aged 12+ need age-appropriate safety and content; homemakers often value everyday scenarios and flexible pacing; interview candidates need focused, repeatable simulations. The MVP does not claim child-directed compliance and should require an appropriate age gate before serving minors at scale.

## Shared constraints

Learners may code-switch, speak with Indian accents, share devices, have noisy surroundings, and fear embarrassment. Designs should use plain language, never equate accent with competence, preserve text fallback, and avoid public comparisons.
